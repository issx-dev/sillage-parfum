# 15. Expansion rule

Do not expand simply because a product exists in the supplier catalog.

Add a new product when at least one of these is true:

- A customer requests it.
- A comparable product sells consistently.
- Search/traffic data shows demand.
- It fills a missing gender/category/price segment.
- It provides a strong premium or niche proposition.
- It complements an existing best seller.

The first objective is **conversion**, not maximum SKU count.

# 16. Conversion strategy — promotions, bundles and packs

The ecommerce should not rely only on individual-product sales. The catalog should support **multi-buy offers, curated packs and threshold-based incentives** designed to increase:

- Average Order Value (AOV)
- Units per order
- Cross-selling
- Repeat purchases
- Conversion rate

Promotions must be configured as a merchandising layer, not baked into the base product prices.

## 16.1 Multi-buy promotions

### Standard 30 ml / 70 ml fragrances

Recommended initial structure:

| Offer | Example | Effective saving | Purpose |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 perfume | 1 × €35 | €0 | Normal purchase |
| 2 perfumes | 2 × €35 → **€63** | €7 | Encourage second item |
| 3 perfumes | 3 × €35 → **€84** | €21 | Encourage basket building |

Recommended customer-facing copy:

- **1 perfume — €35**
- **Llévate 2 y ahorra 7 €**
- **Llévate 3 y ahorra 21 €**

For €18 30 ml products:

| Offer | Bundle price | Saving |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 × 30 ml | €18 | — |
| 2 × 30 ml | **€32** | €4 |
| 3 × 30 ml | **€42** | €12 |

### Important rule

The promotion should generally apply across a **defined eligible collection**, not only to three copies of the exact same perfume.

Example:

> **Combina tus favoritos: 2 por €63**

The customer can choose any two eligible perfumes from the same promotion.

This is substantially more useful for merchandising than forcing the customer to buy duplicates.

---

# 16.2 Mixed-size promotion

Do not initially mix 30 ml and 70 ml into the same simple quantity discount.

Instead, use an upsell:

> **¿Quieres más? Pasa a 70 ml por +X €**

The exact differential should be calculated from the product's actual price.

The objective is to make the 70 ml feel like the obvious value choice without destroying margin.

# 16.5 Cart threshold promotions

Use order-value incentives instead of constant product discounts.

Recommended starting tests:

| Cart value | Incentive |
|---:|---|
| €50+ | Small gift / free shipping |
| €75+ | Better gift / stronger shipping incentive |
| €100+ | Premium gift or stronger benefit |

Do not hard-code these thresholds into product data.

# 16.9 Promotion architecture

Promotions should be represented independently from products.

Recommended conceptual model:

```text
Promotion
├── id
├── name
├── type
├── active
├── startDate
├── endDate
├── eligibleProducts
├── eligibleCollections
├── minimumQuantity
├── discountType
├── discountValue
├── bundlePrice
├── maximumUses
└── priority
```

Possible `type` values:

```text
MULTIBUY
BUNDLE
FIXED_PRICE_BUNDLE
PERCENTAGE
FIXED_DISCOUNT
GIFT
FREE_SHIPPING
THRESHOLD_REWARD
```

---

# 16.10 Promotion rules

1. Never permanently alter the product's base price for a promotion.
2. Show the original total and promotional total clearly.
3. Show the actual saving in euros where possible.
4. Allow promotions to be activated/deactivated.
5. Support start/end dates.
6. Support eligible collections.
7. Prevent incompatible promotions from stacking unless explicitly allowed.
8. Give each promotion a priority.
9. Keep promotion logic separate from product data.
10. Store the discount applied in the order so historical orders remain correct if a promotion changes later.

---

# 16.11 Recommended launch promotions

Do not launch with ten different promotions simultaneously.

Start with **three core mechanisms**:

### Promotion A — Multi-buy

> **2 perfumes → €63**

For eligible €35 fragrances.

### Promotion B — 3-pack

> **3 perfumes → €84**

For eligible €35 fragrances.

### Promotion C — Cart incentive

> **Envío gratis a partir de X €**

`X` must remain configurable.

These three mechanisms are enough to test whether customers respond better to:

- quantity discount
- bundle discount
- order-value incentive

# 16.13 Anti-discount rules

Protect perceived value.

Avoid:

- permanent 30–50% discounts
- fake countdown timers
- fake "was €X, now €Y" pricing
- stacking multiple discounts without a margin calculation
- excessive SALE badges
- discounting premium products aggressively
- showing every product as a promotion

Promotions should create urgency and basket growth without making the normal price look artificial.

---

# 16.14 Data model recommendation

Products should contain the **base commercial price**.

Promotions should determine the effective price at cart/checkout time.

Example:

```text
Product:
  name: Sauvage
  price30: 25.50
  price70: 48.00

Promotion:
  name: "Llévate 2 y ahorra"
  type: MULTIBUY
  minimumQuantity: 2
  bundlePrice: 63.00
```

The cart calculates:

```text
2 eligible products
→ normal total
→ promotion applied
→ saving
→ final total
```

This makes the system extensible for future promotions without modifying the catalog.

# 17. Conversion roadmap

## Phase 1 — Launch

Implement:

- Product catalog
- 30 ml / 70 ml variants
- Best seller badges
- Multi-buy 2 / 3
- Basic packs
- Free-shipping threshold
- Cart upsell
- Gift packaging option

## Phase 2 — After initial sales data

Analyze:

- Average Order Value
- Conversion rate
- Units per order
- Most frequently purchased combinations
- Most viewed products
- Add-to-cart rate
- Promotion redemption rate
- Margin per order

Then create packs from **actual purchase combinations**, not guesses.

## Phase 3 — Optimization

Test:

- 2-for pricing
- 3-for pricing
- percentage vs fixed bundle discount
- free shipping threshold
- gift vs discount
- premium packs
- seasonal packs
- limited-time bundles

The objective is to increase AOV and conversion without training customers to wait for discounts.
